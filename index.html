How often was it higher later?

For each asset, the share of past periods of this length that ended higher than they started, with an honest range around it. It describes what has happened, not what will. Nobody can reliably call the direction of a market, and the self-test alongside shows how little these numbers add.

Read this before trusting a number

  • Past frequency is not future probability. "Up 77% of past years" means exactly that. The next year is one draw, and regimes change.
  • The companies listed are survivors. They are on this list because they grew. Their history flatters them; firms that failed are not here to drag the average down.
  • "Higher" ignores size. An asset can be up most of the time and still lose money if the falls are bigger than the rises. Check the typical range, not only the percentage.
  • The range is real uncertainty. A year-long window overlaps the next one almost entirely, so 25 years of daily data is roughly 25 independent observations, not thousands.
  • Prices are in each asset's own currency and adjusted for splits and dividends. Inflation, taxes, fees and currency moves are not included.
